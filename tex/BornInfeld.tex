  \abstract{
    We organize the bounded-relaxation approach to emergent dynamics around a single structural axiom.
    The capacity axiom [A-cap] states that the per-node relaxation rate of an admissible relational
    configuration is uniformly bounded by a constant $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$.
    The axiom is motivated by non-injective projection together with finite local distinguishability;
    it is not a consequence of non-injectivity alone, which constrains information rather than rates.
    Which continuum quantity inherits the bound is an open problem:
    null field configurations carry arbitrarily large amplitudes at vanishing invariants, so no local
    Lagrangian of the field invariants bounds every constitutive direction.
    Born--Infeld theory is accordingly presented as a saturation candidate, distinguished by its known
    exceptional-propagation properties, not as a derived or unique completion.
    Under explicit hypotheses --- an established continuum limit for the relational Laplacian and the
    existence of a second-order hyperbolic sector --- the admissible signature is Lorentzian
    $(-+++)$, and stationary spherically symmetric flux conservation yields the Newtonian exterior
    profile $\Phi = \Phi_0 - C/r$.
    The Schwarzschild form requires additional dynamical input, and the reading of horizons as
    capacity saturation is relative to a preferred relaxation foliation; both are stated as
    conditional or open rather than as theorems.
    \textbf{Interpretive status.}
    The paper provides a structural scaffold connecting relational capacity to effective continuum
    dynamics.
    Its physical content becomes predictive only when the identified projection bridges --- from
    capacity to constitutive response, from relaxation to hyperbolic dynamics, and from symmetric
    connectivity to gauge curvature --- are closed.
  }

  \section{Introduction}
    \label{sec:introduction}

    A growing body of work explores the possibility that spacetime geometry is not a fundamental
    structure but an effective description emerging from more primitive relational or operator-based
    systems.
    In several approaches, discrete relational data, weighted graphs, or spectral operators admit
    continuum limits in which geometric notions such as distance, dimension, and curvature arise as
    derived quantities rather than postulated ones~\cite{Beau2026a}.
    Within the same programme, non-injective effective descriptions have been analyzed as a source of
    intrinsic limits on observable factorization~\cite{Beau2026b}, and a pre-geometric relational
    framework has been proposed as the underlying substrate~\cite{Beau2026whitepaper}.

    The present paper isolates the load-bearing structural input of the bounded-relaxation approach
    and states precisely what follows from it, under which hypotheses, and what remains open.
    The input is a single axiom: admissible relational configurations relax at a uniformly bounded
    per-node rate.
    We call this the capacity axiom, [A-cap], and we denote the bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, a notation
    that records the Born--Infeld candidate which motivates it and which companion papers of the
    programme use as an input.

    The paper makes five statements, each with an explicit status.
    First, [A-cap] is formulated as an axiom on a named relational variable, motivated --- but not
    implied --- by non-injective projection (Section~\ref{sec:capacity-axiom}).
    Second, the bridge between the discrete capacity bound and any bounded continuum constitutive
    quantity is an open problem, and we explain why no local Lagrangian of the field invariants can
    close it by itself (Section~\ref{sec:constitutive-bridge}).
    Third, Born--Infeld theory is a saturation candidate distinguished by its exceptional-propagation
    properties, and the derivation of such a propagation condition from projection structure is the
    open route to any future selection statement (Section~\ref{sec:bi-candidate}).
    Fourth, under an established continuum limit for the relational Laplacian and under the
    hypothesis of a second-order hyperbolic sector, the effective signature is Lorentzian; the
    hypothesis is necessary, because bounded-flux transport admits finite-speed parabolic
    realizations (Sections~\ref{sec:rel-graph-laplacian-op-and-relaxation-dynamics}%
    --\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence}).
    Fifth, stationary spherically symmetric flux conservation yields the Newtonian exterior profile;
    the Schwarzschild form and the saturation reading of horizons require additional input and are
    presented as conditional statements and interpretation, not as results
    (Sections~\ref{sec:localized-obstruction-and-schwarzschild-geometry}%
    and~\ref{sec:discussion-and-conclusion}).

  \section{The capacity axiom}
    \label{sec:capacity-axiom}

    \subsection{Formulation}
      \label{subsec:capacity-formulation}

      Let $G = (V, E, w)$ be a locally finite weighted graph with symmetric weights
      $w_{ij} = w_{ji} \ge 0$, let $\phi : V \to \mathbb{R}$ be a relational configuration, and let
      $\tau$ be the monotonic ordering parameter of the relaxation process introduced in
      Section~\ref{sec:rel-graph-laplacian-op-and-relaxation-dynamics}.
      The named relational variable of this paper is the \emph{per-node relaxation rate}
      $|\partial_\tau \phi_v|$.

      \medskip
      \noindent\textbf{Axiom [A-cap] (bounded relational capacity).}
      There exists a constant $c_{\mathrm{BI}} > 0$ such that, for every admissible configuration and
      every node $v \in V$,
      \begin{equation}
        \bigl|\partial_\tau \phi_v\bigr| \;\le\; c_{\mathrm{BI}}\,.
        \label{eq:A-cap}
      \end{equation}
      \medskip

      The axiom is stated at the discrete relational level, where the variable it bounds is
      unambiguous.
      Any continuum reformulation requires the bridges discussed in
      Section~\ref{sec:constitutive-bridge} and is not part of the axiom.

    \subsection{Structural motivation from non-injective projection}
      \label{subsec:motivation}

      Let $\Pi : \Omega \to \mathcal{O}$ denote the projection from the relational configuration
      space $\Omega$ to effective observables $\mathcal{O}$, and let
      $\mathcal{E}_y = \Pi^{-1}(y)$ denote the fibre over $y \in \mathcal{O}$.
      When $\Pi$ is non-injective, the fibres are non-trivial and the effective description cannot
      resolve arbitrarily fine relational distinctions~\cite{Beau2026b}.

      Non-injectivity alone, however, does not bound any rate.
      A projection may discard information while the retained variables evolve arbitrarily fast:
      for $\Pi(x, y) = x$ on $\mathbb{R}^2$, the fibres are entire lines and nothing constrains
      $\partial_\tau x$.
      The motivation for~\eqref{eq:A-cap} therefore requires a second ingredient: \emph{finite local
      distinguishability}, the assumption that an effective cell supports only finitely many
      projectively distinguishable states per relaxation step.
      Under finite local distinguishability and locality of the relational dynamics, an unbounded
      per-node rate would transport more distinctions per step than the effective description can
      register, and the excess would be unresolvable.
      This chain motivates the axiom; it does not derive it, because finite local distinguishability
      is itself an assumption about the projection.
      [A-cap] is accordingly an axiom, not a theorem.

      A related structural argument concerns gauge structure.
      If $\Pi$ is injective and compatible with relational locality, each observable configuration
      lifts uniquely, no non-trivial re-identification of fibres exists, and no emergent internal
      symmetry would be available on this account; emergent gauge structure would therefore require
      non-injectivity.
      We use this argument only as motivation for studying non-injective projections.
      Its rigorous formulation requires constructing the projected bundle and its connection, which
      is part of the framework programme~\cite{Beau2026whitepaper} and is not attempted here.

    \subsection{What the axiom does not assert}
      \label{subsec:non-assertions}

      [A-cap] does not assert that any continuum field strength, constitutive response, energy flux,
      or curvature is bounded.
      It does not select an effective Lagrangian.
      It does not fix the relation between $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$ and any continuum scale, including the
      saturation scale $\beta$ of Section~\ref{sec:bi-candidate}.
      Each of these connections is a separate bridge with its own status, examined below.

  \section{The open constitutive bridge}
    \label{sec:constitutive-bridge}

    Suppose a continuum regime exists in which slowly varying effective fields admit a local action
    \begin{equation}
      \mathcal{S} = \int d^4x\,\mathcal{L}\bigl(S, P\bigr)\,,
      \qquad
      S = \tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\,,
      \qquad
      P = \tfrac14 F_{\mu\nu}\tilde F^{\mu\nu}\,,
      \label{eq:local-action}
    \end{equation}
    with Maxwell weak-field limit $\mathcal{L} \simeq -S$.
    The question raised by [A-cap] is which continuum quantity, if any, inherits the discrete
    capacity bound.

    The candidate identifications differ and are inequivalent:
    a bound on the field strength $F$, a bound on the constitutive response
    $D^{\mu\nu} = \partial\mathcal{L}/\partial F_{\mu\nu}$, a bound on the invariants $(S, P)$, and a
    bound on an energy flux are four distinct conditions.
    No local Lagrangian of the invariants bounds every direction at once:
    null configurations satisfy $S = P = 0$ at arbitrarily large field amplitude, so any
    $\mathcal{L}(S, P)$ with a Maxwell limit leaves the response in null directions unbounded.
    Born--Infeld theory itself bounds the purely electric field strength through the reality domain
    of its square root, while leaving null amplitudes unconstrained; it does not bound $F$, $D$, and
    all invariants indiscriminately.

    Consequently, no selection statement of the form ``bounded capacity forces Lagrangian~$X$'' can
    even be formulated until the bounded continuum quantity is specified and derived from the
    projection structure.
    We record this as the first open bridge.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Open Problem 1 (constitutive bridge).}
    Derive from the projection structure which effective continuum quantity inherits the capacity
    bound~\eqref{eq:A-cap}, and in which domain of field configurations the inherited bound is
    active.
    \medskip

    The relaxation-side counterpart of this bridge is the passage from the first-order relaxation
    flow of Section~\ref{sec:rel-graph-laplacian-op-and-relaxation-dynamics} to a second-order
    effective dynamics supporting propagating modes; it is stated as a hypothesis in
    Section~\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence}.

  \section{The Born--Infeld candidate}
    \label{sec:bi-candidate}

    Among nonlinear completions of Maxwell theory, the Born--Infeld
    Lagrangian~\cite{BornInfeld}
    \begin{equation}
      \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{BI}} =
      \beta^2 \left(
                1 - \sqrt{1 + \frac{2S}{\beta^2}
          - \frac{P^2}{\beta^4}}
      \right)
      \label{eq:BI-lagrangian}
    \end{equation}
    is the historical prototype of a saturating theory:
    it reduces to Maxwell theory for $|S|, |P| \ll \beta^2$, its reality domain bounds the purely
    electric field strength by $\beta$, and it assigns a finite self-energy to the point
    charge~\cite{BornInfeld,Gibbons1998}.
    In covariant determinantal form, with an effective metric $g_{\mu\nu}$,
    \begin{equation}
      S_{\mathrm{eff}}
      = \beta^2 \int d^4x
      \left(
        \sqrt{-\det(g_{\mu\nu})}
        - \sqrt{-\det\!\left(g_{\mu\nu} + \frac{1}{\beta} F_{\mu\nu}\right)}
      \right).
      \label{eq:BI-action}
    \end{equation}

    Within the present framework, \eqref{eq:BI-action} is a \emph{candidate}: it is compatible with
    several partial notions of saturation, and it is the candidate whose known structural
    properties best match the intent of [A-cap]
    (Figure~\ref{fig:bounded-vs-linear-flux}).
    It is not derived here, and it is not unique with respect to any bounded-quantity condition
    formulated so far.
    Distinct completions realize distinct partial notions of saturation --- families with a bounded
    reality domain other than the square root, and Lagrangians with saturating response at unbounded
    invariants, both exist --- so that, pending Open Problem~1, no selection theorem is available to
    state.

    What genuinely distinguishes Born--Infeld theory in the nonlinear electrodynamics literature is
    its \emph{exceptional propagation}: among nonlinear local theories $\mathcal{L}(S, P)$ with a
    Maxwell limit, absence of birefringence --- a single characteristic cone for both photon
    polarizations --- singles out the Born--Infeld Lagrangian, Maxwell theory itself being the
    trivial birefringence-free case~\cite{Boillat1970,Plebanski1970}.
    This is a theorem under hypotheses about wave propagation, not about bounds.
    If the projection framework is to select~\eqref{eq:BI-action}, the structural route indicated by
    the literature is therefore to derive an exceptional-propagation condition from the projection
    of relational characteristics.

    \medskip
    \noindent\textbf{Open Problem 2 (propagation bridge).}
    Determine whether the projection structure implies a single-characteristic-cone condition on the
    effective dynamics; if it does, the Born--Infeld candidate is selected by the
    Boillat--Pleba\'nski theorem, and the capacity bound acquires a derived constitutive meaning.
    \medskip

  \section{Relational graph, Laplacian operator, and relaxation dynamics}
  \label{sec:rel-graph-laplacian-op-and-relaxation-dynamics}

  We consider an underlying relational system represented by a weighted graph $G = (V,E,w)$, where
  $V$ denotes a set of nodes, $E$ a set of edges, and $w_{ij} \ge 0$ encodes the strength of the
  relation between nodes $i$ and $j$.
  No embedding in a background spacetime is assumed.
  The graph is taken as a purely relational object, encoding adjacency and coupling structure
  between abstract degrees of freedom.

  A scalar field $\phi_i$ is defined on the nodes of the graph.
  The fundamental operator governing relational variations is the discrete Laplacian,
  \begin{equation}
    (L \phi)_i = \sum_{j} w_{ij} \left( \phi_i - \phi_j \right),
    \label{eq:discrete-laplacian}
  \end{equation}
  which measures the local mismatch of $\phi$ with respect to its relational neighborhood.
  Throughout this work the graph is locally finite and the weights are symmetric,
  $w_{ij} = w_{ji}$.

  The system is assumed to admit an irreversible relaxation dynamics driven by the Laplacian,
  \begin{equation}
    \frac{d \phi_i}{d \tau} = - (L \phi)_i \,,
    \label{eq:relaxation-flow}
  \end{equation}
  where $\tau$ is a monotonically increasing parameter labeling the progression of the relaxation
  process, subject to the capacity axiom~\eqref{eq:A-cap}.
  No interpretation of $\tau$ as a fundamental physical time is imposed; it serves as an ordering
  parameter associated with the irreversible flow toward admissible stationary configurations, and
  the existence of such an ordering distinguishes one direction of evolution from its reverse.

  Equation~\eqref{eq:relaxation-flow} is first order in $\tau$.
  It defines a relaxation flow, not a wave dynamics: by itself it supports no oscillatory
  propagating modes and no finite characteristic cone.
  Whether and how a second-order effective dynamics arises from the relaxation flow is not settled
  in this paper; the signature analysis of Section~\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence} therefore proceeds
  from an explicit hypothesis rather than from~\eqref{eq:relaxation-flow}.

  Stationary configurations satisfy $L\phi = 0$ and correspond to relational equilibria.
  Slowly varying configurations describe regimes in which the system admits an effective
  coarse-grained description whose large-scale observables depend primarily on the spectral
  properties of the Laplacian rather than on the detailed microscopic structure of the graph.
  The graph structure does not imply fundamental discreteness of physical space or time; different
  microscopic realizations with the same large-scale spectral properties are physically equivalent
  within the effective description.

  \section{Conditional continuum limit}
  \label{sec:continuum-limit-and-effective-operators}

  We consider a sequence of weighted graphs $G_N = (V_N, E_N, w^{(N)})$ with $|V_N| \to \infty$,
  satisfying
  (i) uniformly bounded degree growth relative to the intrinsic scaling,
  (ii) local quasi-isotropy of the weighted second moments of adjacency in the low-energy sector,
  and (iii) sufficient decay of $w^{(N)}_{ij}$ with intrinsic relational distance.
  The working hypothesis of this section is that, within a fixed low-energy spectral window, the
  discrete Laplacians converge in the strong resolvent sense to a second-order differential
  operator
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    = \nabla_\mu\!\left(A^{\mu\nu}(x)\,\nabla_\nu\right)
    \label{eq:limit-operator}
  \end{equation}
  on a smooth manifold, with $A^{\mu\nu}$ a symmetric tensor arising from the second weighted
  moments of adjacency.
  The framework-specific formulation of this limit is developed in~\cite{Beau2026a}; its status
  there is that of a reconstruction under stated regularity and density hypotheses, and the present
  paper treats it as a conditional input.

  The established mathematical results closest to this hypothesis concern graphs sampled from, or
  quasi-uniformly discretizing, a Riemannian manifold: pointwise and spectral convergence of graph
  Laplacians is proved in that setting~\cite{BelkinNiyogi2008,BuragoIvanovKurylev2014}.
  These theorems presuppose the manifold whose geometry is recovered.
  An intrinsic convergence statement --- one that does not presuppose a background manifold, as the
  relational programme requires --- is not available in the literature and is open within the
  programme.
  We record the dependence explicitly: every geometric statement below is conditional on the
  continuum limit.

  Under the hypothesis~\eqref{eq:limit-operator}, the principal symbol
  \begin{equation}
    \sigma_2(\mathcal{L})(x,k)=A^{\mu\nu}(x)\,k_\mu k_\nu
  \end{equation}
  provides a coordinate-independent definition of an effective inverse metric,
  $g^{\mu\nu}(x) \propto A^{\mu\nu}(x)$, up to a choice of units~\cite{Beau2026a}.
  The effective metric is a derived object: distinct microscopic graphs with the same principal
  symbol are indistinguishable at the level of effective geometry, and the continuum description is
  defined only up to spectral equivalence within the selected energy window.

  One structural limitation must be stated explicitly.
  The present symmetric weighted Laplacian does not construct an antisymmetric gauge curvature; a
  magnetic or bundle-valued graph Laplacian would be additional structure.
  The gauge field strength $F_{\mu\nu}$ appearing in Section~\ref{sec:bi-candidate} is therefore
  not derived from~\eqref{eq:discrete-laplacian}; its relational origin is a separate construction
  problem.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textbf{Open Problem 3 (gauge-sector bridge).}
  Construct a relational operator --- necessarily carrying more structure than the symmetric
  weighted Laplacian --- whose continuum limit produces an antisymmetric curvature two-form coupled
  to the effective metric, and determine whether the capacity axiom constrains it.
  \medskip

  \section{Conditional signature selection}
  \label{sec:minkowski-emergence}

  This section derives the effective signature from explicit hypotheses.

  \medskip
  \noindent\textbf{Hypothesis [H-hyp] (second-order sector).}
  In the homogeneous low-energy regime, the effective dynamics admits a sector governed by a
  second-order-in-$\tau$ equation of motion whose principal symbol is a non-degenerate quadratic
  form $A^{\mu\nu}_0 k_\mu k_\nu$ on frequency--momentum space $(k_0, \mathbf{k})$, and effective
  propagation is required to exhibit a finite characteristic cone with $\tau$ as the single
  ordering direction.
  \medskip

  [H-hyp] is a hypothesis, for two reasons stated in
  Sections~\ref{sec:rel-graph-laplacian-op-and-relaxation-dynamics}
  and~\ref{sec:constitutive-bridge}.
  First, the underlying relaxation flow~\eqref{eq:relaxation-flow} is first order in $\tau$, and the
  passage to a second-order sector is not constructed here.
  Second, the capacity axiom does not by itself force hyperbolicity: bounded-flux transport admits
  finite-speed \emph{parabolic} realizations.
  Flux-limited (tempered) diffusion equations propagate fronts at finite speed while remaining
  degenerate parabolic~\cite{Rosenau1992}; they are, moreover, structurally of Born--Infeld type.
  A finite characteristic cone therefore does not follow from the capacity bound alone and must be
  demanded of the sector under consideration.

  Under [H-hyp], the signature is fixed, up to overall sign of $A^{\mu\nu}_0$, by a standard
  trichotomy.
  A definite $A^{\mu\nu}_0$ (signature $(++++)$) yields an elliptic operator: no finite
  characteristic cone exists.
  A signature with more than one time-like direction yields an ultra-hyperbolic operator, for which
  the Cauchy problem is not well posed in the classical Hadamard sense~\cite{HormanderPDE}.
  The only remaining possibility is the Lorentzian case, for which the Cauchy problem is well
  posed~\cite{ChoquetBruhat2009}: exactly one negative direction, aligned with the ordering
  parameter $\tau$.
  After normalization of the maximal propagation speed to $c = 1$, the homogeneous effective metric
  is
  \begin{equation}
    g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1, +1, +1, +1)\,.
    \label{eq:minkowski}
  \end{equation}

  The scope of this statement is exactly its hypotheses.
  Conditional on the continuum limit
  (Section~\ref{sec:continuum-limit-and-effective-operators}) and on [H-hyp], homogeneity selects
  flat space with Lorentzian signature, and the null cone $ds^2 = 0$ marks the maximal admissible
  effective propagation rate, in structural parallel with causal saturation in Born--Infeld-type
  theories~\cite{Boillat1970,Gibbons1998}.
  Unconditionally, nothing in this section asserts that the relational substrate produces a
  hyperbolic sector; that is the relaxation-side bridge left open in
  Section~\ref{sec:constitutive-bridge}.

  \section{Stationary radial profiles under spherical flux conservation}
  \label{sec:localized-obstruction-and-schwarzschild-geometry}

  We now consider regimes in which homogeneity is broken by a localized, stationary, spherically
  symmetric obstruction: a region of enhanced relational stiffness whose microscopic origin is left
  unspecified.
  Outside the obstruction the effective operator of
  Section~\ref{sec:continuum-limit-and-effective-operators} takes the form
  \begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}
    = \nabla_\mu \!\left( A^{\mu\nu}(r)\,\nabla_\nu \right),
    \qquad
    A^{\mu\nu}(r) =
    \mathrm{diag}\!\left(-A_t(r),\,A_r(r),\,A_\perp(r),\,A_\perp(r)\right),
    \label{eq:radial-operator}
  \end{equation}
  with the sign structure inherited from Section~\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence}.

  For a stationary scalar probe $\Phi$ of the effective structure, the exterior equation
  $\nabla_\mu (A^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\nu \Phi) = 0$ reduces to radial flux conservation,
  \begin{equation}
    r^{2} A_r(r)\, \frac{d\Phi}{dr} = C\,,
    \label{eq:flux-conservation}
  \end{equation}
  with $C$ an integration constant measuring the strength of the obstruction.
  If the exterior region is asymptotically homogeneous, so that $A_r(r) \to 1$ with the
  normalization of Section~\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence}, the leading exterior profile is
  \begin{equation}
    \Phi(r) = \Phi_0 - \frac{C}{r}
    + O\!\Bigl(\sup_{r' \ge r}\lvert A_r(r') - 1\rvert \; C/r\Bigr)\,.
    \label{eq:newtonian-profile}
  \end{equation}
  This is the Newtonian exterior profile.
  It is the robust content of this section: it follows from stationarity, spherical symmetry, flux
  conservation, and asymptotic normalization, all conditional on the continuum limit.

  The Schwarzschild geometry does not follow from~\eqref{eq:newtonian-profile}.
  A general static spherically symmetric effective line element,
  \begin{equation}
    ds^{2}
    = - f_t(r)\, dt^{2}
    + f_r(r)\, dr^{2}
    + r^{2} d\Omega^{2}\,,
    \label{eq:static-metric}
  \end{equation}
  is constrained by the weak-field identification $f_t = 1 + 2\Phi$ only in its temporal
  coefficient.
  The Schwarzschild form requires in addition the closure $f_t\, f_r = 1$, which is not a
  coordinate choice: it is a dynamical condition, equivalent in the effective-metric language,
  given the asymptotic normalization, to
  the vacuum statement $R^{t}{}_{t} = R^{r}{}_{r}$, and metrics violating it --- for instance
  $f_t = 1 - r_s/r$ with $f_r = 1$ --- satisfy every constraint used in this section.
  Within the programme, dynamical input of this type is supplied, conditionally on independently
  matched coefficients, by the spectral stationarity route of the companion gravity
  papers~\cite{Beau2026h,Beau2026thermo}.
  We therefore state the conditional result only:
  under the hypotheses of this section and, as external dynamical input, the closure
  $f_t f_r = 1$ together with the exact temporal identification $f_t = 1 + 2\Phi$ with
  $\Phi_0 = 0$, the exterior effective geometry is Schwarzschild with $r_s = 2C$ in relational
  units; with the weak-field identification alone, the Schwarzschild form holds only to leading
  order in $C/r$.
  No claim is made that the present framework derives the closure or the exact identification.

  \section{Discussion, status ledger, and outlook}
  \label{sec:discussion-and-conclusion}

  \subsection{Status ledger}
    \label{subsec:status-ledger}

    The statements of this paper have the following statuses.

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Axiom.}
      [A-cap], the uniform bound~\eqref{eq:A-cap} on the per-node relaxation rate.
      Companion papers of the spectral admissibility programme use this bound as an input; stating
      it as a named axiom places their derivations on an explicit, named hypothesis.

      \item \textbf{Structural motivation (not derivation).}
      Non-injective projection plus finite local distinguishability motivate [A-cap]
      (Section~\ref{subsec:motivation}); the injectivity--holonomy argument motivates restricting
      attention to non-injective projections.

      \item \textbf{Computation.}
      The Maxwell weak-field limit of the Born--Infeld candidate, its electric-domain saturation,
      and the finite point-charge self-energy are classical
      results~\cite{BornInfeld,Gibbons1998}.

      \item \textbf{Conditional.}
      Lorentzian signature selection, given the continuum limit and [H-hyp]
      (Section~\ref{sec:minkowski-emergence}).
      The Newtonian exterior profile~\eqref{eq:newtonian-profile}, given the continuum limit,
      stationarity, spherical symmetry, and asymptotic normalization.
      The Schwarzschild form, given additionally the closure $f_t f_r = 1$ from external dynamical
      input.

      \item \textbf{Open.}
      The constitutive bridge (Open Problem~1), the propagation bridge and any Born--Infeld
      selection statement (Open Problem~2), the gauge-sector bridge (Open Problem~3), the
      first-order-to-second-order passage underlying [H-hyp], and the intrinsic (embedding-free)
      continuum limit.
    \end{itemize}

  \subsection{Horizons as foliation-relative saturation: an interpretation}
    \label{subsec:horizon-outlook}

    In the conditional Schwarzschild exterior, the static chart adapted to the relaxation ordering
    degenerates at $r = r_s$: the temporal coefficient $f_t$ vanishes while curvature invariants
    remain finite.
    This degeneracy is chart-relative --- charts regular across $r_s$ exist, and in them the
    effective Cauchy problem continues --- so no invariant breakdown of the effective operator
    occurs at the horizon.
    If, however, the relaxation ordering $\tau$ defines a physically preferred foliation, as the
    relational framework suggests, then the degeneracy of the $\tau$-adapted chart acquires
    structural meaning: the horizon is the locus where the preferred relational slicing ceases to
    be transverse to effective propagation.
    Reading this locus as \emph{capacity saturation} would additionally require a derived relation
    between the local flux scale $C/r_s^2$ and the capacity bound $c_{\mathrm{BI}}$, which is not
    available.
    A complementary reading in terms of projection entropy --- the growth of fibre multiplicity
    limiting local resolvability --- is developed in the non-injectivity
    analysis~\cite{Beau2026b}.
    Both readings are interpretation, not results, and we record them as outlook.

  \subsection{Relation to neighbouring programmes}
    \label{subsec:positioning}

    Three bodies of work delimit the present contribution.
    In nonlinear electrodynamics, the selection theorems of Boillat and
    Pleba\'nski~\cite{Boillat1970,Plebanski1970} single out the Born--Infeld Lagrangian among
    nonlinear completions by exceptional propagation; the present programme shares the goal of
    structural selection but must
    \emph{derive} the selecting condition from projection structure rather than postulate it, which
    is precisely Open Problem~2.
    In discrete-to-continuum analysis, spectral convergence of graph Laplacians is established for
    graphs built on an underlying manifold~\cite{BelkinNiyogi2008,BuragoIvanovKurylev2014}; the
    relational programme requires an intrinsic counterpart, and until one exists the geometric
    statements here remain conditional on the framework-specific limit of~\cite{Beau2026a}.
    In transport theory, flux-limited diffusion~\cite{Rosenau1992} realizes bounded flux with
    finite-speed fronts in a parabolic setting, demonstrating that a capacity bound does not entail
    hyperbolicity and thereby fixing the honest status of [H-hyp] as a hypothesis.

  \subsection{Interpretive outlook}
    \label{subsec:interpretive-outlook}

    \textbf{Interpretive status.}
    The structural reading of this paper is that a single finite capacity, if it governs the
    relational substrate, would leave three recognizable signatures in effective physics: a
    saturating constitutive response, a preferred Lorentzian causal structure, and
    saturation-limited strong-field regions.
    The paper shows that each signature currently stands at the end of an open bridge ---
    constitutive, dynamical, and geometric respectively --- and formulates the bridges precisely.
    Closing any one of them would convert the corresponding signature from a motivated reading into
    a derived consequence; failing to close them would confine the capacity axiom to the discrete
    relational level.
    This is the calibrated sense in which bounded relational capacity may, but does not yet,
    explain why saturation structures pervade effective physics.
